Computational Cognitive Science Training Portfolio

February 2026 · Active

Overview

This repository documents a structured progression of practical training in Python programming, scientific computing, statistical analysis, mixed-effects modeling, Bayesian modeling, and reproducible research workflows. Rather than presenting a single research study, it serves as a cumulative record of methodological development for computational cognitive science. The portfolio emphasizes research-oriented data analysis, probabilistic reasoning, hierarchical modeling, transparent workflows, and reproducible computational methods as preparation for future empirical and computational research.

Learning Objectives

The primary objective of this project is to develop the computational, statistical, and modeling foundations required for behavioral and cognitive science research. The training focuses on scientific programming, data processing, statistical inference, mixed-effects modeling, Bayesian analysis, visualization, and reproducible analytical workflows that support experimental psychology and computational modeling.

Methods and Skills

The portfolio combines practical exercises with progressively more advanced analytical workflows. Topics include data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, classical statistical inference, regression analysis, mixed-effects modeling, longitudinal data analysis, generalized linear mixed models, Bayesian inference, hierarchical Bayesian modeling, posterior predictive checking, model comparison, numerical computation, and scientific visualization using Python’s scientific computing ecosystem. Practical case studies introduce mixed-effects and Bayesian approaches to trial-level cognitive psychology data, culminating in complete end-to-end analyses using both frequentist and Bayesian multilevel models. Throughout the portfolio, emphasis is placed on reproducibility, transparent implementation, and principled interpretation of statistical uncertainty.

Methodological Principles

All work in this repository follows a consistent set of methodological principles:

  • Reproducible analytical workflows.
  • Transparent data processing and documentation.
  • Rigorous statistical and Bayesian inference supported by diagnostic evaluation.
  • Clear separation between raw data, preprocessing, analysis, and outputs.
  • Simulation-based learning before application to real datasets.
  • Interpretation based on uncertainty and statistical evidence rather than point estimates alone.

Current Scope

The portfolio currently covers Python programming, scientific computing, data manipulation, scientific visualization, classical statistical analysis, mixed-effects models, Bayesian statistics, hierarchical Bayesian modeling, Bayesian model evaluation, simulation-based design analysis, longitudinal modeling, generalized linear mixed models, and introductory applications to cognitive and neural modeling. The repository is intended as a structured training portfolio rather than a collection of independent research projects.

Next Steps

Future development will extend the portfolio toward advanced computational cognitive science topics, including hierarchical cognitive process models, reinforcement learning, computational neuroscience, and analyses of real experimental and neuroimaging datasets.

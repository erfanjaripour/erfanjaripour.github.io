## Overview

This repository documents a structured progression of practical training in Python programming, scientific computing, statistical analysis, and reproducible research workflows. Rather than presenting a single research study, it serves as a cumulative record of methodological development for computational cognitive science. The portfolio emphasizes research-oriented data analysis, transparent workflows, and statistical reasoning as preparation for future empirical and computational research.

## Learning Objectives

The primary objective of this project is to develop the computational and statistical foundations required for behavioral and cognitive science research. The training focuses on scientific programming, data processing, statistical inference, visualization, and reproducible analytical workflows that support experimental psychology and computational modeling.

## Methods and Skills

The portfolio combines practical exercises with progressively more advanced analytical workflows. Topics include data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, statistical inference, regression analysis, numerical computation, and scientific visualization using Python's scientific computing ecosystem. All exercises emphasize reproducibility, transparent implementation, and interpretation of results within a research context.

## Methodological Principles

All work in this repository follows a consistent set of methodological principles:

Reproducible analytical workflows.
Transparent data processing and documentation.
Statistical inference supported by assumption checking.
Clear separation between raw data, analysis, and outputs.
Interpretation based on statistical evidence rather than numerical results alone.

## Current Scope

The portfolio currently covers the computational foundations of behavioral data analysis and statistical modeling. It does not yet include Bayesian inference, hierarchical modeling, cognitive process models, machine learning, or large-scale experimental datasets. These topics are planned as future extensions.

## Next Steps

Future development will expand the portfolio to include Bayesian statistics, probabilistic programming, hierarchical models, cognitive process modeling, reinforcement learning, and computational modeling techniques commonly used in contemporary cognitive science research.

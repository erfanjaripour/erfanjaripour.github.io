title: "About"

My name is Erfan Jaripour. I am a computational cognitive psychology researcher. My focus is on memory, learning, and language-related mechanisms in human cognition. My work combines behavioral analysis, statistical modeling, and computational approaches. It addresses my desire to understand the core cognitive mechanisms. I select to study how cognitive processes are structured, how they interact, and why they sometimes fail under conflict, load, or interference.

My research orientation is based on mechanisms rather than descriptions. I am most interested in questions that ask not only what people do, but why they do it and what internal process could produce that behavior. I am especially drawn to problems where cognition can be studied at the intersection of representation, limitation, and change over time. My interests include memory storage and interference, learning dynamics, and the role of memory constraints in language processing. I prefer empirical research with theoretical coherence and computational explanatory potential.

I work in a reproducible Python-based workflow. My approach emphasizes careful data cleaning, transparent preprocessing, subject-level and trial-level behavioral analysis, effect size estimation, and statistical inference. I also use computational simulation when it helps clarify whether a simple mechanism can reproduce observed behavioral patterns. I value methods that are rigorous but interpretable, and I try to keep my analyses traceable from raw data to final figure.

My current research interests center on a small set of connected themes: memory and working memory, learning and memory–learning interaction, language processing under memory constraints, and computational modeling of cognitive mechanisms. These themes are not separate directions; they are parts of a single research trajectory.

My current projects are designed to build a coherent profile in computational cognitive science through reproducible analysis, project-based inquiry, and progressively deeper theoretical work. I am developing a research portfolio that is intended to be methodologically serious, conceptually focused, and suitable for advanced graduate training.
